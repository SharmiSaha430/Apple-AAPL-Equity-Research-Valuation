# Apple (AAPL) — Equity Research & Valuation Model

**Company:** Apple Inc. (NASDAQ: AAPL)  
**Research report date:** April 30, 2026  
**Investment view:** HOLD

## Overview

This independent equity research project evaluates Apple's financial performance, intrinsic valuation, market pricing, and downside risk. The accompanying Excel workbook brings together historical financial statements, operating forecasts, several valuation approaches, and an investment discussion.

## What's in the Excel model

- **Financial statement analysis:** Historical income statements, balance sheets, and cash flow statements.
- **Operating assumptions:** Product unit volumes, average selling prices, and revenue drivers.
- **Discounted cash flow (DCF):** Forecast free cash flows, terminal value, and valuation scenarios.
- **Cost of capital:** CAPM inputs and weighted average cost of capital (WACC).
- **Comparable-company analysis:** Relative valuation using peer multiples.
- **Football field analysis:** Comparison of valuation ranges across approaches.
- **Risk analysis:** Historical return distribution, Value at Risk (VaR), and a 10,000-iteration Monte Carlo simulation.
- **Investment discussion:** Apple's ecosystem, Services business, AI-related expectations, capital returns, and valuation risks.

**How to interpret the target:** The $298.50 figure represents an illustrative scenario of approximately 10% appreciation from the $271.35 reference price. It is **not** calculated from the DCF or peer valuation. The gap between the market price and the lower fundamental valuations is a central issue explored in the investment discussion. The workbook includes assumptions and analyses with their own reference dates; see the relevant worksheets for details.

This is an independent educational portfolio project, **not investment advice**. Valuations are assumption-dependent estimates, not forecasts guaranteed to occur. The stated prices and conclusions relate to the model's historical research context and are not live recommendations. For sources, detailed calculations, and model limitations, refer to the Excel workbook.
